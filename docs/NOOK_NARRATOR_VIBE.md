# Nook Narrator Vibe

Date: 2026-06-11
Status: active style brief for Hanging Gardens nook narration and short environmental text

## Purpose

The Hanging Gardens narrator should make each nook feel transient, dangerous, and memorable.

The player is not receiving a lore lecture. They are brushing against evidence that a life was lived here under pressure.

This document is the tone brief for:

- nook TTS lines
- short inscription fragments
- hint-sized environmental narration
- future one-sentence district or landmark voice lines

## Influence Boundary

Do not try to imitate any living author directly.

The target is a set of high-level qualities:

- transient: every refuge feels temporary
- dangerous: survival always costs something
- memorable: one line should leave a mark
- anthropological: people are understood through habits, rites, and residue
- severe but humane: the world is hard, but the narration is not sneering
- compressed: one sentence should do the work of a paragraph

## Narrator Position

The narrator is not a guide, historian, or poet performing for the player.

The narrator sounds like a witness moving through remains:

- close to material detail
- aware of ritual, law, hunger, and violence
- unsentimental about death
- attentive to what people chose to preserve
- brief enough to feel like a strike, not a monologue

The narrator should sound as if this world has many dead, many rules, and very little time.

## Core Effect

Every good nook line should imply three things at once:

1. What happened here.
2. What kind of people these were.
3. What pressure shaped the act.

A line that only names props is too flat.
A line that only explains lore is too abstract.
A line that carries act, culture, and pressure is the target.

## Sentence Shape

Default form: one sentence, one image, one turn.

Preferred length:

- 7 to 18 words for the sharpest TTS lines
- 8 to 22 words for written fragments when clarity needs a little more room

Good sentence structures:

- object, then meaning
- act, then consequence
- custom, then threat
- count, then absence
- rule, then wound

Examples of useful turns:

- a civic object becomes a survival tool
- a household habit becomes a taboo
- a memorial practice becomes evidence of mass loss
- a child lesson reveals political fear
- a repair note reveals scarcity and triage

## Diction Rules

Prefer:

- plain nouns
- hard verbs
- material details
- civic and ritual vocabulary
- specific counts when they matter
- east, bridge, cistern, bell, ash, rope, seal, veil, ledger, step, bowl, handprint

Avoid:

- dreamy fantasy phrasing
- decorative adjectives stacked together
- generic darkness words like `ancient`, `eldritch`, `haunting`, `mysterious`
- over-explaining motive
- rhetorical questions
- modern irony
- therapy language
- blog cadence

## Emotional Rules

The line can be sad, but it should not beg for sadness.

Prefer:

- restraint
- implication
- finality
- pressure
- social memory

Avoid:

- melodrama
- sentimental pity
- purple apocalypse language
- announcing the theme instead of embodying it

## What Makes A Line Hit

A line becomes memorable when it contains contrast under pressure:

- tenderness inside siege logic
- law inside collapse
- ritual inside scarcity
- children inside public danger
- ordinary domestic order beside mass death

The Hanging Gardens should often sound like civic life continuing after the civic body has already been wounded beyond repair.

## Nook Priorities By Family

### Shelter

Should imply:

- temporary safety
- rank or sleeping order
- concealment
- exhaustion

Best pressure words:

- shift
- edge
- hidden
- dawn
- waiting

### Shrine

Should imply:

- substitution
- broken rite
- withheld names
- witness or bargain

Best pressure words:

- vow
- knot
- mercy
- witness
- unblessed

### Repair

Should imply:

- triage
- scarcity
- repurposing
- practical intelligence under stress

Best pressure words:

- splice
- patched
- marked twice
- first
- salvaged

### Ration

Should imply:

- counting
- unequal shares
- quiet enforcement
- household survival math

Best pressure words:

- seal
- share
- counted
- closed
- portion

### Burial

Should imply:

- routine death
- dignity under shortage
- naming rules
- institutional or familial memory

Best pressure words:

- wrapped
- ash
- rung
- witness
- confirmed gone

## TTS-Specific Rules

For spoken nook lines:

- one sentence only
- no semicolon unless the sentence absolutely needs it
- no parenthetical clauses
- no second sentence for explanation
- strong stop at the end
- write for one breath when possible

If a line reads like setup plus explanation, cut the explanation.
If a line sounds like synopsis, replace it with an image and a pressure.

## Compression Test

Before approving a line, ask:

- Can one clause be cut?
- Can one abstract noun become a material noun?
- Can the final phrase land harder?
- Does this sound spoken, or merely written?
- Would the player remember the image ten minutes later?

If not, compress again.

## Good Targets

These are the kinds of lines we want:

- `The toll ledger ended at the poisoning.`
- `This grafting table became a splinting bench.`
- `Each white handprint marks one witness.`
- `The bell fell before any of these names were rung.`

Why they work:

- one image
- one event or rule
- strong noun anchor
- no wasted framing
- wider history implied but not explained

## Bad Targets

These are the kinds of lines to avoid:

- `This was once an important place where the survivors came to remember those they had lost.`
- `People here were very afraid, so they developed a lot of customs around silence and mourning.`
- `The atmosphere of the cistern was filled with grief and mystery.`

Why they fail:

- summary instead of strike
- explanation instead of residue
- no dangerous edge
- no memorable object

## Final Rule

If a line sounds like background lore, it is wrong.
If it sounds like a scar with a civic memory attached, it is close.
